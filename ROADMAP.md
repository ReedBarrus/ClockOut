# Roadmap

**Status:** Provisional evidence-gated roadmap. `Product_Thesis.md` is the sole current product authority.

**Purpose:** Describe how observed work and real use may justify increasing ClockOut's complexity without turning projected capabilities into established product architecture.

> ClockOut grows outward from the minimum capability required for one person to own and operate their livelihood. Each phase must produce an observable improvement in operator agency or customer clarity before additional complexity is justified.

## Evidence Chain

```text
observed work
→ tested dual-surface loop
→ live utility
→ earned boundaries
→ cross-operator pressure
→ usable commons
```

The phases are evidence gates, not a feature backlog. A phase progresses only when its observable success condition is met and its progression gate is supported by evidence.

## Governing Rules

- The first implementation is a candidate Core, not an earned Core.
- Townsend Electric is the first pressure specimen, not the product ontology.
- Basic capacity legibility belongs in the first usable loop.
- Rich workload modulation comes only after real use demonstrates the necessary distinctions.
- Ownership, exportability, provider replaceability, and customer-relationship ownership constrain the product from the first live slice.
- Existing external systems remain authoritative where appropriate until evidence warrants an adapter or native replacement.
- General adapter contracts must not be invented before recurring integration pressure exists.
- Manual handoffs are acceptable when explicit and visible.
- Optional complexity is not a roadmap phase. It is admitted only when an observed failure justifies it.
- No capability becomes Core merely because it appeared useful in a mockup or one exceptional specimen.
- The first Townsend pilot must be reversible and must not require abandoning existing business channels.

## Initial Pressure

> Can ClockOut help Townsend Electric turn existing or newly generated local demand into visible, actionable potential work without increasing operator administrative burden?

This pressure does not assert a specific Townsend failure. Phase 1 must establish where consequential failures, burdens, or ambiguities actually occur.

## Provisional First Live-Loop Boundary

```text
existing discovery surface
→ ClockOut Storefront / work-request boundary
→ request becomes visible in Cockpit
→ operator explicitly chooses a next action or commitment
```

Communication, scheduling, payment, accounting, and other activities may remain in existing external systems during this first slice.

## Phase 1 — Townsend Electric Reality and Baseline

### Purpose

Observe Townsend Electric's actual work and establish the baseline, first consequential pressure, and reversible trial boundary without assuming how the business operates.

### Evidence / Observations Required

- How recent real jobs were discovered.
- How inquiries currently arrive.
- How Matt decides whether work is a fit.
- How commitments are made.
- How scheduling currently happens.
- What factors actually determine capacity.
- Which systems currently carry communication, scheduling, payment, and customer history.
- Where attention or expectations actually become unclear.
- What “enough work” means operationally.
- What current manual handoffs exist and whether they are visible and reliable.
- A reversible first trial boundary that preserves existing business channels.
- Baseline evidence against which success and failure can be judged.

Observation of recent real work outranks hypothetical workflows, generic industry assumptions, and desired future behavior.

### Observable Success Condition

The current journey can be reconstructed from real examples; consequential uncertainty, burden, and breakdowns are distinguishable from assumptions; the first trial has a bounded target and falsifiable success and failure conditions.

### Why This Phase Comes Now

The repository contains a thesis and projected direction but no Townsend specimen evidence. Mocking or implementing before observation would risk treating one imagined workflow as the product.

### Explicitly Outside Scope

- Selecting product architecture or implementation technology.
- Declaring a Core, adapter contract, or generalized service-work model.
- Assuming which Townsend problem ClockOut must solve.
- Replacing current business systems or channels.

### Progression Gate to Phase 2

Proceed only when real examples support a provisional customer journey, operator journey, initial pressure, trial boundary, and comparison baseline.

## Phase 2 — Coupled Storefront–Cockpit Prototype

### Purpose

Test the Storefront and Cockpit as one connected operating loop using real Townsend situations before committing the loop to implementation.

### Evidence / Observations Required

- Real request examples and decision points from Phase 1.
- The minimum information a customer needs to understand the service, expectations, request path, and what happens next.
- The minimum information Matt needs to notice, qualify, and choose a next action for a request.
- The basic capacity consequence needed to evaluate a request in Townsend's actual terms.
- Operator and, where available, representative customer walkthroughs.
- Points where an explicit manual handoff to an existing system remains sufficient.

### Observable Success Condition

Representative real scenarios can be walked through from Storefront request to an explicit operator next action or commitment. The customer-facing expectation and operator-facing decision are clearer than in the baseline, and the prototype exposes the smallest information and actions required for the loop.

### Why This Phase Comes Now

Phase 1 supplies actual vocabulary, constraints, and failure pressure. A coupled prototype can test the minimum loop more cheaply and reversibly than a live implementation.

### Explicitly Outside Scope

- Production implementation.
- Full scheduling, communication, payment, accounting, or follow-up workflows.
- General adapter design.
- Rich workload modulation.
- Treating prototype convenience as proof of Core status.

### Progression Gate to Phase 3

Proceed only when the prototype survives real Townsend scenarios, the operator can identify the request and choose a next action, customer expectations are legible, and the live slice can be stated without speculative supporting capabilities.

## Phase 3 — Smallest Live Townsend Operating Loop

### Purpose

Deploy the smallest reversible implementation that can test whether real demand becomes visible and actionable without increasing operator administrative burden.

### Evidence / Observations Required

- Real requests crossing the provisional live-loop boundary.
- Whether requests remain visible from arrival through an explicit next action or commitment.
- Whether the operator can understand what needs attention, what has been decided, and the request's basic capacity consequence.
- Whether customers understand what happens after submitting a request.
- Administration introduced, removed, or shifted by ClockOut.
- Manual handoffs and the external systems that remain authoritative.
- Evidence that ownership, exportability, provider replaceability, and customer-relationship ownership are preserved by the slice.

### Observable Success Condition

Real Townsend requests enter through the Storefront boundary, become visible in the Cockpit, and receive an explicit operator next action or commitment. The loop improves request visibility or decision clarity without materially increasing administrative burden, preserves basic capacity legibility, and leaves existing business channels available.

The slice is unsuccessful if it materially increases administrative burden without improving request visibility or decision clarity, if requests remain easy to lose, if commitments remain unclear to the operator, or if the customer still cannot understand what happens next. Numerical thresholds remain unset until the baseline supports them.

### Why This Phase Comes Now

The tested prototype provides a bounded candidate for implementation. Only live operation can establish utility, burden, and recurring boundary pressure.

### Explicitly Outside Scope

- Claiming that the implementation is an earned Core.
- Rebuilding communication, scheduling, payment, accounting, or other external systems.
- General-purpose adapters or speculative native replacements.
- Advanced capacity optimization or demand modulation.
- Requiring Townsend Electric to abandon existing channels.

### Progression Gate to Phase 4

Proceed only when repeated live use provides enough evidence to distinguish useful capability from one-off convenience, identify recurring external-system pressure, and describe which capacity distinctions affect actual decisions.

## Phase 4 — Evidence-Earned Core, Adapter, and Capacity Boundaries

### Purpose

Classify what repeated live utility earns as Core, what remains specimen-specific, what belongs to external systems, where an adapter is justified, and which richer capacity distinctions have operational consequence.

```text
candidate capability
→ repeated live utility
→ earned Core
```

### Evidence / Observations Required

- Repeated Townsend use across varied real requests.
- Capabilities whose absence causes recurring loss of agency or customer clarity.
- Capabilities used only in exceptional cases.
- Repeated manual handoffs and their actual burden or failure modes.
- Which external systems remain suitable authorities and where their state must become legible in ClockOut.
- Capacity distinctions that repeatedly change qualification, commitment, or scheduling decisions.
- Evidence that the candidate boundaries preserve ownership and reduce rather than relocate administration.

### Observable Success Condition

Every promoted Core capability has repeated live evidence and a stated consequence. Core, specimen configuration, external authority, manual handoff, and any justified adapter are distinguishable. Richer capacity behavior is added only where observed decisions require it.

### Why This Phase Comes Now

Core and adapter boundaries cannot be earned from the thesis, a mockup, or a single exceptional request. The live Townsend loop supplies the recurring pressure needed for classification.

### Explicitly Outside Scope

- A universal ontology for independent service work.
- General adapter contracts based on anticipated integrations.
- Native replacement of an adequate external system.
- Capacity features not tied to observed operator decisions.
- Optional complexity justified only by potential usefulness.

### Progression Gate to Phase 5

Proceed only when the candidate has repeated live utility, promoted Core claims are evidence-backed, remaining uncertainty is explicit, and there is a sufficiently bounded model for another operator to falsify.

## Phase 5 — Cross-Operator Falsification

### Purpose

Pressure the earned and candidate boundaries with additional operators to discover which assumptions are general, Townsend-specific, or still unsupported.

### Evidence / Observations Required

- Operators selected because they exert useful pressure on qualification, scheduling, delivery, capacity, payment, or customer expectations.
- At least one meaningfully different operating journey rather than only superficial industry variation.
- Evidence of what transfers without change, what belongs in operator-specific configuration, and what fails.
- Attempts to use the existing loop without expanding it merely to accommodate every difference.
- Continued evidence for ownership, portability, and replaceable external systems.

Automation or vibecoding may be considered as a later specimen, but it is not preselected. Specimens are chosen for the assumptions they test.

### Observable Success Condition

Additional operators can attempt the operating loop, and the result clearly falsifies, narrows, or supports the proposed Core boundary. Townsend-specific assumptions do not silently become universal product structure.

### Why This Phase Comes Now

A working, evidence-backed candidate must exist before transfer can be meaningfully tested. Earlier generalization would abstract from imagined similarities.

### Explicitly Outside Scope

- Precommitting to particular industries or operator types.
- Expanding ClockOut into a universal business platform.
- Rebuilding every external function encountered in another business.
- Treating one successful transfer as proof of universal applicability.

### Progression Gate to Phase 6

Proceed only when cross-operator evidence has either supported a small common boundary or narrowed it honestly, and the project can state what is shared, configurable, external, and unresolved.

## Phase 6 — Genuinely Usable Commons

### Purpose

Make the pressured ClockOut core genuinely usable as open-source infrastructure by someone outside the founding work.

### Evidence / Observations Required

- Attempts by people outside the founding work to obtain and understand ClockOut.
- Independent operation and modification without privileged project knowledge.
- Demonstrated export of operator data and operating history.
- Demonstrated retention of domain and customer relationships.
- Demonstrated replacement of connected providers or integrations where applicable.
- A demonstrated path to leave ClockOut without losing the operating history of a livelihood.

### Observable Success Condition

Someone outside the founding work can obtain, understand, operate, modify, export from, and leave ClockOut. The software supports ownership in practice rather than only through stated intent.

### Why This Phase Comes Now

Making an unpressured abstraction easy to distribute would harden speculative structure. Townsend use and cross-operator falsification must first establish what belongs in the commons.

### Explicitly Outside Scope

- Feature completeness across every kind of independent work.
- Mandatory managed hosting or commercial services.
- A bundled replacement for every connected external system.
- Complexity added only to make the project appear platform-complete.

### Progression Gate

The commons is ready for broader use only when an independent person can complete the ownership tests above and discovered barriers are either resolved or explicitly bounded. Further complexity remains subject to the same evidence gates.

## Intentionally Unresolved Until Observation

- The most consequential current Townsend failure or burden.
- The precise meaning of capacity and “enough work” for Townsend Electric.
- The exact information required at the Storefront request boundary and in the Cockpit.
- Which current systems are authoritative for each part of the relationship.
- Which manual handoffs are adequate and which create recurring pressure.
- Numerical success and failure thresholds for the first trial.
- Whether any adapter or native capability is required.
- Which later operators will provide the most useful cross-operator pressure.

These questions are inputs to evidence gathering or later gates. They are not permission to fill missing knowledge with generalized business-software assumptions.

## Decisions Withheld for Lack of Evidence

This roadmap does not select frameworks, schemas, databases, hosting providers, APIs, integrations, UI architecture, implementation technologies, generalized adapter contracts, detailed feature backlogs, automated capacity models, or speculative product layers. Those decisions are not yet supported by the product evidence.
