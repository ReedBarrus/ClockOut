
1. Proposed Core v0 guarantees
1. Operator-controlled Storefront and request boundary
   One operator can define and revise the work they offer, relevant service boundaries, identity, and initial customer expectations through a publicly reachable Storefront.
2. Customer comprehension
   A potential customer who reaches the Storefront can understand who the operator is, what work is offered, whether initiating a request appears appropriate, how to request it, and what happens immediately afterward.
3. Honest request passage
   A submitted request becomes durably visible to the operator in the Cockpit. ClockOut must not report successful receipt when the request has not crossed that boundary.
4. Attention and explicit operator agency
   The operator can distinguish requests needing attention from requests with an explicit next action. They can clarify, decline, accept, defer, or record another appropriate disposition. Acceptance creates an explicit commitment distinguishable from an unaccepted request.
5. Expectation continuity
   The operator can see what the customer was told, and the customer can understand the current next step. When responsibility moves to an external channel, that handoff is explicit rather than silently implied.
6. Basic capacity legibility
   When deciding what to do with a request, the operator can see or record the capacity consequence that actual Townsend observation establishes as decision-relevant.
7. Coexistence, ownership, and reversibility
   Existing business channels may remain authoritative. The operator retains their domain, customer relationships, and the business and operating data held by ClockOut; that data is portable. The pilot can be stopped without preventing Townsend Electric from continuing through its existing channels.
8. Operational usefulness
   The live loop must improve request visibility, decision clarity, or customer clarity without materially increasing operator administrative burden merely to reproduce existing work.
2. Observable acceptance criteria
Guarantee	Observable acceptance criteria
Operator-controlled Storefront	Matt can represent and revise Townsend’s actual offering without adopting an invented industry model. He recognizes the published representation as accurate. At least one real discovery route can lead a customer to the Storefront.
Customer comprehension	A representative potential customer reaching the Storefront can correctly explain who provides the work, what is offered, relevant fit boundaries, how to initiate a request, and what follows submission.
Honest request passage	A customer can submit a request and receive confirmation only when the corresponding request is operator-visible. Matt can find it during a later normal use session. A failed submission is visibly unsuccessful and does not create false confidence that the operator received it.
Attention and operator agency	Matt can identify which requests need him. He can record an explicit next action or disposition without being forced into unsupported lifecycle states. An accepted request is visibly distinct from one still under consideration.
Expectation continuity	The post-request expectation shown to the customer is also legible to Matt. After a decision or handoff, Matt can identify what happens next, who is responsible, and whether the next step occurs through ClockOut or an external channel. A representative customer can state the same next expectation.
Basic capacity legibility	At the point of decision, Matt can recognize or record the request’s capacity consequence using the distinctions found in Phase 1. He can explain how that consequence informed the decision. No generalized capacity model is required.
Coexistence, ownership, and reversibility	Existing communication, scheduling, payment, and accounting practices can continue. External handoffs are identifiable. Matt can obtain the business, request, decision, and commitment information ClockOut holds, retain his domain and relationships, and stop the pilot without losing the ability to operate the business.
Operational usefulness	Compared with the Phase 1 baseline, requests are harder to lose or decisions and expectations are clearer. If ClockOut materially adds administration without producing one of those improvements, Core v0 fails. The numerical meaning of “materially” must be established from the baseline rather than invented now.


3. Proposed non-guarantees / explicitly external outcomes
Business outcomes ClockOut may help produce and measure, but cannot guarantee:
- More qualified local requests.
- More accepted jobs.
- A higher proportion of desirable work.
- Reduced time spent handling inquiries.
- Improved conversion from inquiry to commitment.
- Better alignment between incoming work and available capacity.
- The source and progression of demand, where that evidence is actually available.
External outcomes ClockOut does not guarantee:
- Search ranking or placement.
- Website, directory, social, referral, or advertising traffic.
- Advertising performance.
- A particular volume of requests, jobs, revenue, or utilization.
- Customer truthfulness, responsiveness, suitability, or willingness to buy.
- Customer trust merely because information is displayed.
- Operator availability, response speed, acceptance, or delivery quality.
- Scheduling, communication delivery, payment, or accounting outcomes carried by external systems.
- Legal enforceability of a recorded commitment.
- Accuracy of operator-entered information or externally maintained information.
Discoverability therefore requires split contract language:
- Core v0 obligation: provide a credible, operator-controlled, customer-reachable Storefront and functioning request boundary.
- Deployment acceptance: demonstrate that at least one real Townsend discovery route reaches that boundary.
- External business outcome: whether enough people discover it and become qualified work remains measurable but not guaranteed.
4. Guarantees to split, narrow, remove, or postpone
- “Become discoverable” — split. Guarantee reachability and a usable public boundary; do not guarantee discovery volume or acquisition performance.
- “Credible Storefront” — narrow. Guarantee accurate operator-controlled representation and observable customer comprehension, not universal trust or perceived legitimacy.
- “Define the work they want” — narrow. Core v0 must express one operator’s real offering in their terms. It need not model every possible service business.
- “Persistently legible” — make falsifiable. Define an agreed retention expectation and require honest failure behavior. Do not leave “persistent” indefinite.
- Clarify, decline, accept, defer — avoid a closed taxonomy. Require an explicit next action or disposition; preserve only distinctions demonstrated by real use.
- Requested, considered, committed, scheduled, active, completed, and other states — postpone most. Core v0 needs attention, explicit action, and commitment distinctions. Later states enter only if the first loop cannot be useful without them.
- “Commitment” — narrow. It means a legible operator decision and associated expectation, not a legal contract, scheduled appointment, completed job, or payment obligation.
- Capacity — split. Basic decision-relevant consequence is required now. Forecasting, optimization, automatic scheduling, and demand modulation are postponed.
- Portability and provider replacement — stage. Core v0 must preserve data, domain, relationships, and reversibility. Full independent distribution, modification, and self-hosting usability belongs to the later commons phase.
- “Get Matt jobs” — remove as a guarantee. It is a desired business consequence and pressure measure, not controllable product behavior.
- General adapter behavior — postpone. Core v0 must make handoffs explicit; recurring live pressure must justify any adapter contract.
5. Missing guarantees without which the first loop fails
- No false-success guarantee: a customer must not be told a request was received unless it became operator-visible.
- Public/private separation: a potential customer must not be able to access the private Cockpit, operator capacity, other customer requests, or internal commitments.
- Correction: the operator must be able to correct obsolete or inaccurate offer, expectation, request-context, or decision information under their control.
- End-to-end correspondence: the operator-visible request must correspond to what the customer submitted closely enough that both parties are discussing the same potential work.
- Failure legibility: when ClockOut cannot complete its portion of the loop, the affected person must be able to tell that the loop did not complete and what safe next step remains.
6. Valuable capabilities explicitly not required for Core v0
- Native email, phone, messaging, or unified inbox.
- Native calendar or scheduling.
- Payments, invoicing, or accounting.
- Automated qualification, follow-up, or marketing.
- Agents or human-takeover systems.
- Rich customer portals or chat.
- Live visitor activity or advanced attribution.
- Search optimization or advertising management.
- Automatic demand modulation.
- Detailed capacity forecasting or workload optimization.
- Full requested-to-paid lifecycle tracking.
- Generalized adapter contracts.
- Multiple operators, teams, or staff coordination.
- A universal service-business model.
- Independently usable self-hosting and modification workflows required of the eventual commons.
7. Smallest unresolved questions before writing the contract
1. What does Townsend Electric actually offer, and what minimum facts let a real customer judge whether requesting it is appropriate?
2. Which real discovery route will demonstrate that the Storefront is reachable without making acquisition performance a product guarantee?
3. What information must a Townsend request contain to be actionable, and what confirmation can honestly be given at submission?
4. What observable distinction separates “needs attention,” “next action chosen,” and “commitment made” in Matt’s current practice?
5. What customer expectation must be communicated at submission and after an operator decision, including when communication moves elsewhere?
6. Which actual factors determine Townsend’s capacity decisions?
7. Which current systems remain authoritative, and which manual handoffs must be made explicit?
8. What persistence and export boundary is sufficient for the pilot to preserve operating history and make reversibility real?
9. What baseline establishes whether administrative burden, request visibility, decision clarity, and customer clarity improved or worsened?
